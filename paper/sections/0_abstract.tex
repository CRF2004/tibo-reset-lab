\begin{abstract}
Rare public operational actions are easy to narrate after they occur and difficult to forecast
without leaking post-event information. We study a single auditable stream: public announcements of
special Codex usage-limit resets. The dataset contains 41 accepted announcements, 315 daily periods,
1,260 six-hour periods, and prediction-safe official incident contexts. Expanding-window historical
experiments compare global and adaptive event rates, renewal features, calendar features, and an
official-incident model. A 30-day rolling rate is the strongest daily historical baseline
(Brier 0.119664), outperforming the calendar model (0.125443); at six-hour resolution the calendar
model reaches 0.036443. Official-incident features do not provide stable incremental improvement,
and paired block-bootstrap intervals include zero. We therefore freeze a broader baseline set and establish an immutable prospective
protocol in which discovery, primary-source verification, prediction, outcome scoring, exclusion,
and revision occupy separate append-only layers. Fault drills pass eight predefined failure cases,
and an auxiliary audit identifies heterogeneous application outcomes among 20 self-selected public
reports, although only one has an exact timestamp. This draft reports historical diagnostics and the
prospective design, not prospective superiority. Formal model comparison will occur only after at
least 180 valid scheduled days and 20 prospective positives.
\end{abstract}
